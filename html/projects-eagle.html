Eagle Scout Project | Phoenix, AZ (2018-2019)

For my Eagle Scout project, I addressed a flooding and biohazard problem at a local horse rescue in the Phoenix area: an unusually rainy season had left their parking lot collecting standing water, creating unsafe, unsanitary conditions for the horses and volunteers. I worked with civil engineers to survey the lot and draft a regrading plan, then organized volunteers from my community to redistribute the dirt so the lot would drain properly into the nearby storm drains.

I also fundraised for gravel to pave the lot afterward, which helped prevent the erosion that had caused the flooding in the first place. Leading that project — from the engineering survey through fundraising and coordinating volunteer labor — is part of what earned me the rank of Eagle Scout on May 16, 2019.

Scouts BSA's Eagle Scout rank requires more than fulfilling merit badges — the capstone project has to demonstrate that a Scout can plan, fundraise, and lead a team through a real project that benefits their community, without doing the physical work alone. My project meant coordinating with the rescue's staff and outside civil engineers before a single volunteer showed up.

Only a small percentage of Scouts who join Scouts BSA go on to earn Eagle, and it remains one of the experiences I point to most when I talk about where my leadership habits come from — planning a project end-to-end, communicating with stakeholders outside my own team, and staying accountable for a result rather than just a task.
